---
name: Monsters & Aliens
description: Unknown creatures, alien beings, and experiments gone wrong, survived in a sealed-off place. For stories set on space stations, in research labs, on lonely islands, deep in the mountains, or in towns under invasion.
---

## Style
- The monster is heard, smelled, and known by its traces before it is ever seen clearly: dragged slime, a torn-open vent, a radio cut off mid-word.
- Write concrete physical sensations and surroundings: cold sweat, ragged breath, alarm lights, dwindling ammunition and battery.
- Action scenes are fast and tight: short sentences, sounds, a sense of distance. In quiet moments, write the oppressive wait.

## The world and common elements
- The monster follows consistent biology: afraid of light or heat, hunting by sound or smell, able to parasitize or mimic. Its weaknesses are found only through observation and sacrifice.
- The sealed-off place is the star: a space station, an Antarctic base, an underground lab, a town under quarantine, exits cut off and rescue far away.
- What drives the plot: a hidden experiment log, an infected companion, the last escape pod, the choice of whether to sacrifice someone.
- Suspicion and self-interest among humans are often more dangerous than the monster: someone wants a live specimen, someone hides that they've been bitten.

## Tying into the game rules
- This is the genre with the heaviest physical threat: scratches, bites, broken bones, and blood loss are body conditions, and parasitism or infection should visibly get worse over time.
- The inventory matters: weapons, ammunition, flashlights, medkits, and keycards run out or get lost, and scarcity should force the protagonist into trade-offs.
- Long hours of hiding and watching companions die add mind conditions (panic, exhaustion, despair).

## Pacing
- Establish everyday life and the characters first, then let the first encounter shatter everything; no head-on clash in the opening.
- Alternate hiding, fleeing, and brief respite. The monster doesn't appear every turn; it's scariest when unseen.
- Every success has a cost: a wound, lost supplies, or a lost companion.

## Clichés to avoid
- The protagonist gets a heavy weapon and mows the monsters down.
- The monster's abilities change whenever the plot needs them to.
- Companions as nameless background lining up to be eaten, with no choices of their own.
