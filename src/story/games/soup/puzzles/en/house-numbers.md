## title
Prices at the Hardware Store

## surface
The hardware store owner says: "One costs ten dollars, twelve costs twenty, and a hundred and fifty costs thirty." The customer thinks for a moment, buys a hundred and fifty, and pays thirty dollars.

## truth
The customer was buying the digits for a house number, ten dollars per digit. "12" is two digits, so twenty dollars; "150" is three digits, so thirty. He had just moved into number 150 and needed the numbers for his door.

## key_points
- He was buying house-number digits
- The price is per digit, ten dollars each
