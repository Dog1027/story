## Style
- Saturate the city's senses: neon, rain, holographic ads, the smell of cheap synthetic food, noise that never stops.
- Snappy dialogue with a bit of slang ("the badges", "net ghost", "back-alley clinic", "chrome"), but never so much that readers get lost.
- Write about the costs and inequality technology brings, not how it works.

## The world and common elements
- Power belongs to the corporations: corporate security outguns the police, data is worth more than lives, and everyone owes someone money.
- What drives the plot: a stolen data chip, someone who needs extracting, a debt that can't be repaid, a deleted memory.
- Implants have a price: rejection, overload, getting hacked, debt. Show it with body conditions (neural burns, implant failure) and mind conditions (dissociation, paranoia).
- Be concrete about gear: hacking rigs, burner ID chips, black-market drugs, trackers. Who owns an item and who's tracing it both matter.

## Common set pieces (pick some)
A rainy-night handoff at a street stall, sneaking into a corporate tower, getting traced back mid-hack, surgery at a back-alley clinic, sold out by the client, a rooftop standoff with drones.

## Pacing
- The job is always more complicated than promised: every act can bring a new employer or a new traitor.
- In quiet moments, write human warmth: an old friend's shop, a hot bowl of soup, a corner the corporations haven't bought yet.

## Clichés to avoid
- The protagonist hacks into any system effortlessly.
- The corporation as a vague "evil empire": give it specific executives and specific interests.
- Nothing but gunfights: give the player chances to negotiate, trade, and betray.
