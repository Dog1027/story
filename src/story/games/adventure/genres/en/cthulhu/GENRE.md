name: Cthulhu
description: Unspeakable horror, forbidden knowledge, and a mind slowly coming apart. For stories investigating uncanny events, ancient cults, remote towns, and secrets of the deep sea.
---

## Style
- Fear comes from suggestion, not description: smells, sounds, shapes in the wrong proportions, the way people avoid your eyes.
- Reveal the truth in fragments: torn diary pages, hearsay, murals, dreams. Never fully explain "what it was".
- A calm, restrained voice, like an account written afterward; the closer to the truth, the less steady the narration.

## The world and common elements
- Ordinary places hide extraordinary things: a dying fishing village, a university library, a cellar, the reef bared at low tide.
- What drives the plot: a document that shouldn't exist, a missing friend or relative, a symbol that keeps recurring.
- Humans are small: the protagonist can stop a ritual, flee, or seal something away, but never defeat what truly lies beyond.

## The mind is the heart of it
- The more the protagonist sees, reads, and understands, the more mind conditions to add or worsen (fear, hearing things, paranoia, sleeplessness).
- Mind conditions must affect judgment: misremembered details, doubting companions, seeing what others can't.
- Bodily harm is not the main threat. Moments of rest can ease mind conditions, but never quite back to how they were.

## Pacing
- Build unease slowly: the first turns are investigation and small wrongnesses in daily life. No monsters right away.
- Every revelation has a price; leave something slightly off even in calm scenes.

## Clichés to avoid
- The protagonist shoots the tentacled monster dead and wins outright.
- Naming "Cthulhu" outright, or treating the gods as villains you can reason with.
- Gore and shock in place of atmosphere.
