## Style
- Fear comes from something slightly wrong in everyday life: an extra pair of slippers, knocking at midnight, one person too many in a photo, a radio changing stations by itself.
- Bring in local folk belief and practice, explained in passing: Ghost Month (the seventh lunar month), joss paper burned for the dead, birth-date charts, soul-calming rites, divination blocks, spirit mediums, ghost marriages, temple keepers and Taoist priests. Make them feel like rumors you'd really hear.
- Tell it like a ghost story passed by word of mouth: "they say…", "only later did anyone learn…". What's left unsaid is scarier than describing the ghost.

## The world and common elements
- Ghosts have rules: don't look back, don't answer, don't look in a mirror after midnight. Reveal the rules in the story first; consequences come only when one is broken.
- Every ghost has a life story and an obsession: a wrongful death, an unfulfilled wish, a forgotten promise. Finding out who it is and why it stayed matters more than driving it away.
- What drives the plot: a house that is unreasonably cheap, something picked up and brought home, a funeral left unfinished, a family secret the elders won't speak of.
- Exorcism has a price: talismans, rites, and a god's help all cost something, and may not work.

## Tying into the game rules
- Running into the uncanny, being followed, or seeing what shouldn't be seen adds mind conditions (fright, a dazed spell, sleeplessness, the feeling of being possessed), growing worse the closer the truth.
- Ghosts rarely cause severe wounds directly; body conditions tend to be fever, coldness, unexplained bruises. The real danger is being lured somewhere dangerous.
- A soul-calming rite, praying at a temple, or leaving the cursed place can ease mind conditions; an amulet, incense ash, or a keepsake in the inventory can matter at the crucial moment.

## Pacing
- Build from small things: the first turns are odd noises and coincidences the protagonist can shrug off, until they can't be explained away.
- Every investigation uncovers a little more of the dead person's past; the climax is usually facing them, and fulfilling or refusing their obsession.

## Clichés to avoid
- A ghost that bursts in gory and chases the protagonist around, turning it into a monster movie.
- A priest who subdues the ghost in one move, with no cost and no reason.
- Explaining every strange event away as a misunderstanding or a human trick, so the supernatural stops mattering (unless that is a carefully planned twist).
