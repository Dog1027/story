---
name: Wuxia
description: Feuds of the martial world, rival sects, chivalry and revenge. For stories of duels, pursuits, hunts for secret manuals, escort missions, and debts of gratitude.
---

## Style
- A slightly old-fashioned, formal register: "this humble one", "honored senior", "young lady", "sir". No modern words (phones, kilometers, OK).
- Short, forceful sentences. In fights, describe the spirit of the techniques and the turning point of the duel, not a blow-by-blow list.
- Characters often go by titles or nicknames, e.g. "the Iron-Faced Judge", "the Lady in White"; a title should hint at their temperament or martial art. Personal names are romanized Chinese (Shen Qingyi, Liu Hanyan).

## The world and common elements
- The martial world (jianghu) is made up of sects, great families, gangs, and the court's hounds, bound by old grudges, pacts, and trades of interest.
- What drives the plot: a secret manual, a bloody massacre, a promise, a feud within a school.
- Martial arts have a price: internal injuries, qi deviation, poison, damaged meridians, shown as body conditions; severe wounds need a famed physician or a rare elixir.
- Reputation and honor matter: breaking faith or betraying one's master earns the scorn of the martial world, and saving a life is repaid one day.

## Common set pieces (pick some; don't cram them all in)
A brawl at an inn, a duel on the arena stage, an ambush while camping in the wild hills, hunting for an antidote, an enemy in disguise, loyalty and betrayal among sworn friends, a trial set by one's school.

## Pacing
- After a fight, give room to breathe: tending wounds, sharing wine and confidences, trading information.
- Enemies shouldn't just get stronger; give them their own stand and reasons. The greatest enemy is often someone familiar.

## Clichés to avoid
- The protagonist suddenly awakens a peerless art and beats everyone with one move.
- Everyone fights over the same manual without reasons of their own.
- Women who exist only as decoration or hostages.
